01 — The answer

244 supplier bills were marked "ignored" instead of allocated

The accounting integration pulled 477 supplier invoices into Wunderbuild. None are sitting unprocessed — the queue is clear. But 244 of them were marked ignored rather than imported against a job, and they total $395,414 inc-GST. That is not a filing error at the margins. It is most of the missing cost side of the business.

Twenty-one of them are $5,000 or more, totalling $233,165. These are not ambiguous overheads — they are unmistakably job costs:

The habit appears to have stopped around April 2026

The ignored bills cluster between mid-2025 and April 2026. Everything after that is being processed. So this is a historical backlog to clean up rather than an active daily leak — which is considerably better news than the headline number suggests, and it means someone changed how this is handled. The backlog still needs working through, because it holds the cost history for every completed job.

Not all 244 belong to a job

Some will be legitimate exclusions — duplicates, credits, and genuine overheads like office supplies that shouldn't sit against a job. The list needs a review pass, not a bulk import. Working it in batches by job is the only safe way: a bill allocated to the wrong job moves real money between two jobs' P&Ls and corrupts both.

06 — Fastest fix

46 timesheets, one action, real cost booked

The unprocessed timesheets show zero hours and zero rates, which looks like broken data. It isn't. Hours are computed from start and end times, and rates resolve from the rate cards, at the moment of processing. The 62 sheets already processed prove it — they carry real hours and rates of $40–45/hr, and have booked $23,486 between them.

07 — Order of work

What to do, fastest first

  1. Process the 46 timesheets using default rates. One action, books roughly $14–17K of labour cost, and needs no decisions. Check the rate cards first — a worker showing $0 is a blocker, not a number to invent.
  2. Review the 244 ignored bills in batches by job. Start with the 21 over $5,000 — that's $233,165 and the fastest way to give the completed jobs a real cost history. Expect some to be legitimately ignored; don't bulk import.
  3. Find Gabriel Miller's costs. Fully invoiced, fully paid, zero cost recorded. It's the smallest job on the list and the clearest test of whether the ignored bills explain the gap.
  4. Then, and only then, look at margin. Every margin number in the system today is unreliable. Once capture is above 90%, the estimate-integrity check becomes worth building — and you'll finally know whether 20% pricing is holding.
